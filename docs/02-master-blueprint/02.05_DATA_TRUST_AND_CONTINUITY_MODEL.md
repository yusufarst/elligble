# MB-05 — Data Trust & Continuity Model

**Status:** LOCKED v1.0.0  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-16

## 1. Document Authority, Status, Purpose, and Scope

This document (MB-05) operates under the canonical Decision Hierarchy (`docs/00-governance/00.02_DECISION_HIERARCHY.md`) and the constraints established by LOCKED predecessors (MB-01 through MB-04). It does not override or silently contradict those established definitions.

The canonical purpose of MB-05 is to establish **conceptual data integrity and lifecycle rules**, focusing strictly on data ownership, provenance, longevity, and continuity across the platform.

This document is conceptual. It defines **WHAT** trusted-state and continuity properties must hold. It explicitly does **NOT** define **HOW** technical persistence, database schemas, or system architecture implements them (which are deferred to Architecture / Build).

## 2. Data Trust Principles

ELLIGBLE enforces the following foundational trust principles:

*   **ONE DOMAIN OWNS THE TRUTH:** Each critical concept has a single authoritative domain owner. Other domains may consume authorized information or derived projections, but they do not silently become authoritative owners of that truth.
*   **CONCEPTUAL CLASSIFICATION:** Information is strictly classified into distinct conceptual states:
    *   **Authoritative / Source Truth:** The active, primary record owned by its governing domain.
    *   **Derived / Projection State:** Data aggregated, summarized, or transformed for downstream consumption (e.g., Track signaling).
    *   **Historical Snapshot:** An immutable point-in-time record that cannot be silently altered (e.g., Application Snapshot).
    *   **Lifecycle / Context State:** The active context governing a relationship (e.g., Enrollment status).
    *   **Governed Evidence / Provenance:** Verifiable context that establishes trust and authority (e.g., Passport record).

## 3. Identity Continuity

The core identity rules are preserved from established boundaries: **Person ≠ User Account ≠ Membership**.

*   **Person is Durable:** A Person represents a real human and maintains a stable, continuous identity across their ELLIGBLE lifecycle (including account deactivation, school transfer, graduation, or multiple concurrent memberships).
*   **Account Activation/Deactivation:** Deactivating or altering a User Account does not delete or destroy the underlying Person identity.
*   **Membership & Transition Continuity:** Changes in Membership (e.g., moving to a new school, graduation, or assignment shifts) reflect changing contexts. They do not result in the creation of a new, separate Person.

*Note: Identifiers, primary keys, identity matching, and specific account merge logic are explicitly excluded from MB-05.*

## 4. Organization / Tenant / Membership Continuity

The core organizational rules are preserved: **Organization ≠ Tenant**.

*   **Context-Specific Membership:** Membership represents a specific, time-bound relationship between a Person and an Organization/Tenant. 
*   **Tenant Isolation:** Tenants maintain strict operational isolation for academic and operational data.
*   **Historical Continuity Boundaries:** While a Person's identity is continuous, historical association with one Tenant does not imply automatic cross-tenant data movement or unrestricted authorization in another Tenant.

*Note: Exact mechanics for transfer, data deletion, archival storage, or reactivation across Tenants remain deferred to MB-06/Architecture or are OPEN.*

## 5. Academic Truth Continuity

The Academic Core is the canonical owner of shared academic truth.

*   **Scope:** Academic Core governs enrollment context, academic year/period, grade/program/major/concentration, subject/offering, Academic Group (Rombel), and Teaching Assignment. It also owns selected official academic result projections (where canonically defined).
*   **Boundaries:** Academic Core is **NOT** a full SIS/ERP. General school timetable scheduling and general school-day attendance tracking remain **OUT OF CURRENT BASELINE**.
*   **Continuity:** Academic Core provides the authoritative truth for downstream consumption (e.g., Secure Assessment eligibility). Downstream systems consume projections but cannot overwrite the authoritative source.

## 6. Secure Assessment Trust & Continuity

Secure Assessment guarantees are mission-critical. MB-05 preserves the following distinctions:
**Student ≠ Exam Participant ≠ Exam Attempt ≠ Exam Session**  
**Assessment Type ≠ Exam Instance**  
**Question Bank Item ≠ Immutable Exam Question Snapshot**

*   **No Lost Answers:** Exam Sessions must preserve responses continuously. Temporary connectivity loss or session interruptions must not result in the silent loss of submitted answers or the creation of an ambiguously active replacement Attempt.
*   **Server-Authoritative Timer:** Timers are governed by server-side trust, preventing client manipulation.
*   **Idempotent Submission:** A submitted Attempt reaches a terminal state and must not become ambiguously active again.
*   **Historical Context:** The Exam Question Snapshot is immutable and acts as the frozen context for historical assessment results.

*Note: MB-05 does not design ACK sequencing, cache, persistence models, or reconciliation algorithms (deferred to MB-07/Architecture).*

## 7. Track / Early Warning / Care Boundary

Preserve strict data separation: **Early Warning = Track signal ≠ Care Case**.

*   **Track Signaling:** Track generates derived insights (Early Warnings) but does not independently define or mutate Care truth.
*   **Care Context:** A Care Case only arises through an authorized referral or intake process. 
*   **Continuity Limitation:** Only the minimum necessary referral context crosses the boundary conceptually from Track to Care. Downstream workflows (Care) return high-level safe workflow statuses (e.g., "Under Review") to Track, preventing the leakage of private counseling notes or sensitive details.

## 8. Passport Provenance / Trust Boundary

Preserve: **Profile ≠ Portfolio ≠ Passport**.

*   **Trusted Portable Record:** The Passport is governed and trusted. It must retain identifiable provenance context so that the source and authority of the record remain distinct and verifiable.
*   **No Silent Upgrades:** Unverified Profile or Portfolio content must not silently become trusted Passport truth without an authorized, governed endorsement process.
*   **Provisional Trust:** **Passport Trust-Level Rules remain PROVISIONAL.** MB-05 explicitly does NOT finalize trust scores, the universal verifier, Endorser roles, credential hierarchies, endorsement workflows, or cryptographic mechanisms.

## 9. Path Boundary

Preserve: **Goal ≠ Target ≠ Action** and **Readiness ≠ Eligibility**.
Preserve: **Relevant ≠ Eligible ≠ Recommended**.

*   **Consumption without Mutation:** Path may consume authorized, selected information from Passport or Academic Core to present goals and targets. Path cannot mutate upstream authoritative truth (e.g., altering a Passport record).
*   **Open Mechanics:** The exact Path formula, matching algorithms, and scoring models remain OPEN and are excluded from MB-05.

## 10. Application Snapshot Continuity

Preserve: **Application Snapshot ≠ Live Passport**.

*   **Historical Integrity:** An Application Snapshot represents an immutable, application-specific historical context captured at the time of submission.
*   **Continuity:** Subsequent changes to the live Passport must not silently rewrite or alter the historical Application Snapshot. The source and provenance context of the snapshot remain frozen.

## 11. Partner / Verified Connection / Outcome Continuity

Preserve: **Partner Organization ≠ Partner Staff**.
Preserve: **Opportunity ≠ Application ≠ Verified Connection ≠ Outcome**.

*   **Explicit Transitions:** An Application does not automatically convert into a Verified Connection, nor does a Connection automatically become an Outcome. These transitions require explicit, governed confirmation.
*   **Purpose-Limited Access:** Partner access is authorization-aware. Historical continuity of an outcome does not grant a Partner unrestricted live access to student data or search pools.

*Note: Final retention, revocation impacts, consent-withdrawal mechanics, and exact outcome verification processes remain unresolved (OPEN) or deferred.*

## 12. Alumni Continuity

Preserve: **Student → Alumni = Same Person lifecycle/context**.

*   **Unified Identity:** Graduation represents a transition in lifecycle context, not the creation of a second Person or a duplicate alumni-specific Passport/Path identity.
*   **Trusted History:** Appropriate trusted history is maintained across the graduation transition, strictly bound by canonical evidence. 

*Note: Exact post-graduation access limits and retention mechanics are explicitly not invented here.*

## 13. Support / Audit / Provenance Boundary

Preserve: **Support Case ≠ Platform Incident**.

*   **Shared Accountability:** Audit serves as a shared platform capability to ensure accountability and provenance tracking for critical transitions.
*   **Conceptual Requirement:** High-stakes changes (e.g., Exam submission, Care referral, Passport endorsement) require conceptual provenance traceability.

*Note: MB-05 does not design audit tables, event sourcing, SIEM, or technical incident infrastructure.*

## 15. Provenance Model — Conceptual Only

Provenance in ELLIGBLE is the conceptual ability to understand the origin, authority, and historical basis of trusted information. Conceptual dimensions of provenance include:
*   **Source Domain:** Which domain created the record (e.g., Secure Assessment).
*   **Originating Context:** The specific event or state that generated it (e.g., Exam Submission).
*   **Governing Authority:** The actor or rule that verified it (e.g., Teacher grading, System timer).
*   **Lifecycle State:** Whether the record is active, historical, or archived.
*   **Verification/Governance State:** The degree of trust established (relevant primarily to Passport).

*Note: This model explicitly prohibits defining provenance database schemas, cryptographic signatures, blockchain implementations, or specific logging structures.*

## 16. Cross-Domain Continuity Examples

Canonical ownership dictates that data flowing across boundaries does not transfer ownership authority:
*   **Learn → Track:** Appropriate finalized or derived learning progress flows to Track. Track cannot edit Learn's authoritative truth.
*   **Secure Assessment → Track:** Only finalized/approved results flow to Track. Raw anti-cheating evidence remains isolated in Assessment.
*   **Track → Care:** Data flows only via authorized referral.
*   **Track → Passport:** Only meaningful, portable, and governed records are promoted to Passport records.
*   **Secure Assessment → Passport:** Routine exams do not automatically become governed Passport entries.
*   **Passport → Path:** Path consumes selected authorized information but cannot edit the Passport source.
*   **Opportunity Application:** Uses an Application Snapshot rather than linking to an unrestricted, mutable live Passport.
