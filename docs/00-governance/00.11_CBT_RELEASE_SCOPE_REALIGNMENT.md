# Current Release Scope Realignment: Secure Assessment / CBT

## 1. Decision

The Owner has narrowed the current release of ELLIGBLE to **Secure Assessment / CBT only**, labelled **CBT-R1**: a production-ready, mobile-first web CBT platform for one school, planned and verified for about 1,000 concurrent students using Android phones, iPhones (iOS Safari), Android tablets, iPads and school PCs or laptops, deployed on the school's existing domain and VPS.

CBT-R1 is a release boundary, not a change of product identity. DEC-001 (multi-tenant platform) and DEC-007 (one school = one academic tenant) remain in force: CBT-R1 runs one academic tenant on an architecture that keeps tenant isolation. No single-school shortcut may weaken tenant, membership, assignment or object-level authorization.

## 2. Problem, priorities and invariants

The problem is trusted digital assessment: no silent answer loss; recovery on unstable networks; local-first durable answers; a server-authoritative timer; idempotent submission; controlled session and device transfer; reliable concurrent execution; browser-observable risk signals; live supervision; auditable supervisor and operator actions; trustworthy results.

Engineering priority order (Owner): (1) answer and data integrity, (2) exam reliability, (3) security and authorization, (4) correctness, (5) device and browser compatibility, (6) operational simplicity, (7) concurrency and performance, (8) maintainability, (9) visual polish, (10) architectural novelty. Correctness and answer safety are never traded for speed.

The Secure Assessment invariants of the Architecture exit gate stay binding, among them: server-authoritative timer; idempotent submission; a locally pending answer is not a server-acknowledged answer; reconnect is not retake; Participant ≠ Attempt ≠ Session; immutable exam question snapshots; a risk signal is not a cheating verdict.

Teacher and Proctor authority are not automatically equivalent. Authorization is default-deny and enforced on the server at tenant, role and context, exam, room, participant and object level, including the authority for high-risk actions. Frontend visibility is never authorization.

Terminology: in repository code and the runbook, "operator" means the **platform operator** (audited provisioning CLI). "School Operator" always means the SCHOOL_OPERATOR assignment.

## 5. Student identity and account activation (Owner policy)

- Student identity (Person plus official school identity data), login Account, registration claim and verification are separate concepts (D02.3-01, D02.3-02).
- A student may initiate registration. That creates a claim only, never a trusted identity or an active account.
- A claim is matched against school-authoritative data imported by the School Operator (D02.3-20, D02.7). An ambiguous match is never merged automatically (D02.7-17, D02.7-18).
- Activation or verification is performed by the HOMEROOM_TEACHER within their rombel, or by the SCHOOL_OPERATOR.
- Activation, rejection, recovery, reset and every identity-sensitive action are audited (D02.5-14).
- The existing activation-card path (migration `0039`, platform operator CLI) stays valid.
- Matching keys, stored attributes, display rules and recovery proof follow §12 (OD-05, OD-06) and are made exact in the P2 identity specification.

Identity invariants (Owner, LOCKED):

- One official Student identity has at most one active Account.
- One Student identity cannot hold several active or pending claims that could produce duplicate accounts.
- Uniqueness and concurrency safety are guaranteed at the persistence layer (database constraints, enforced transactionally), not only by UI or application pre-checks.
- Account recovery never creates a second Student identity or a second account. A forgotten account is recovered through the school-assisted reset workflow (D02.5-12).

## 5A. Participant room reassignment (Owner requirement)

An authorized School Operator can move a participant's room assignment from one exam room to another, including while the exam is running, with a mandatory reason, explicit confirmation and an append-only audit trail.

Reassignment preserves:

- the Student identity;
- the Participant;
- the Attempt;
- accepted answers;
- the local and server reconciliation semantics;
- the authoritative remaining time;
- the submission state.

Reassignment never:

- creates a retake;
- creates a new Attempt;
- deletes or resets answers;
- resets the timer.

This extends D04.4-27 (one current room per participant; historical room transfers stay recorded) and D04.4-28 (a room transfer keeps the same participant, Attempt, exam snapshot, answers and randomization). The safe transition and the session behaviour during reassignment are specified in P2 and P3.

## 6. Risk signals and supervision (Owner policy)

Browser or device event → risk signal → human review → incident or supervisor action where appropriate → academic or disciplinary decision outside automatic inference.

Signals are contextual evidence, never proof (D04.7-02, D04.7-04, D04.7-30, D04.7-32, D04.7-33). Each capability carries an honest support state per runtime environment (D04.7-12, D04.7-13). The default preset, the signal set and incident handling follow §12 (OD-08) and are made exact in the P2 risk-signal specification.

## 9. CBT Release Gate

CBT-R1 may go to production only when all of the following hold:

- **G1 Tasks:** every Task in the approved P11 baseline is DONE with recorded evidence; none is BLOCKED, IN_PROGRESS or VERIFYING.
- **G2 Defects:** no known P0 defect or P1 release blocker; no known tenant-isolation violation, authorization bypass, answer-loss path or duplicate-account path; no production mock or fake dependency; no visible interaction that is not implemented.
- **G3 Answer integrity (PB-07):** the Zero-Lost-Answer verification artifact is complete (offline, reload, browser restart, session or device takeover, room reassignment during a running exam, server restart, expiry, pause and lock, submission storm), each case checked against the answers stored on the server.
- **G4 Capacity:** load and performance evidence on production-equivalent infrastructure meets the P6 budgets for the CBT-R1 concurrency profile (§10). No capacity claim is made without it.
- **G5 Devices (PB-06):** the critical journeys pass on real devices of the agreed matrix, with platform limits recorded honestly.
- **G6 Security:** the CBT permission matrix (PB-05) is approved and enforced on the server with negative tests; production security headers and edge limits are deployed; no secret is in the repository; the incident owner and contacts are recorded (PB-12).
- **G7 Privacy and legal:** PB-01, PB-02, PB-03 and PB-10 are closed with evidence before real student data is loaded.
- **G8 Operations (PB-11):** TLS on the school's domain; monitoring and alerting active; automated backups with an encrypted off-host copy; the restore drill executed on the production infrastructure; operational interventions only through audited paths; runbook current.
- **G9 Release:** migrations and the release rehearsed on staging; UAT sign-off by the school for the Student, Teacher, Homeroom Teacher, Proctor and School Operator journeys; rollback target known; the release declares its database change and its recurring-cost change.
- **G10 Cost:** no new paid recurring dependency without explicit Owner approval.

## 10. Non-functional targets (initial; quantified in P1 and P6)

- **Concurrency profile:** about 1,000 concurrent students in one school exam period, including login and start bursts, answer-save bursts, reconnect storms, submission storms, server restart and recovery, database contention, rate limiting behind school NAT or shared addresses, timer and status polling, and supervisor and operator dashboards.
- **Devices and browsers:** Android Chrome; iOS Safari on iPhone; Android tablet; iPad Safari; Chrome and Edge on desktop. Real-device evidence where real browser behavior matters; responsive widths alone are not sufficient.
- **User interface:** Bahasa Indonesia only in CBT-R1; mobile-first, responsive, adaptive, accessible per the design system, calm under exam conditions; explicit state, clear feedback, confirmation and reason for high-stakes actions.
- **Hosting and cost:** the school's own domain and VPS; additional recurring production cost near zero; paid services only with explicit Owner approval.

## 11. Production data safety (Owner rules)

Ordinary planning and coding agents never mutate production data: no reset, truncate, wipe, reseed, destructive manual SQL, ad-hoc production repair, drop or recreate of the database, or destructive volume cleanup. Schema changes go only through versioned migrations tested outside production, rehearsed on staging, with backup readiness, approval and a controlled release. Operational interventions use audited product or CLI paths; the manual-SQL session revocation in `docs/production/OPERATIONS_RUNBOOK.md` §6 is replaced by an audited path before go-live.
